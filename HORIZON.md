## Scope

This file sets out the time horizon of the UniCORE AI reference architecture — publication readiness of the 12-level stack, reference-implementation status, and the horizon for full-conformance audits and broad multi-sector adoption. The canonical horizon statement is published in the TrueAI repository at [`HORIZON.md`](https://github.com/bryanunitek/TrueAI/blob/main/HORIZON.md). This file extends that statement to the architecture level.

## What is ready today

- The **UniCORE AI** reference architecture — the 12-level governance stack, inter-level messaging protocol, reasonable governance threshold specification, human override protocol — is published in full.
- The **TrueAI Foundation** invariants that the architecture implements are stable, published, and citable.
- Architecture documents, scenario library, and implementation guidance are open for use under CC BY 4.0.

## What is not ready today

- No known AI system is currently certified as fully conformant to the UniCORE AI 12-level architecture.
- Prototype implementations exist and are being built openly, but they are prototypes under active development, not shipped products.
- No independent audit body yet exists to assess conformance claims against the architecture.

**This is expected.** Reference architectures for consequential decision-making are not proven at launch. They are proven by prolonged implementation across many systems, many sectors, and many adversarial conditions.

## Time horizon

The architecture's time-to-maturity is measured in decades, not quarters. A realistic horizon for implementation conformance:

- First partial implementations of the 12-level stack reaching production use in narrow domains: **1–3 years**.
- First implementations demonstrating full-stack conformance under independent audit: **5–10 years**.
- Broad multi-sector adoption with a functioning audit ecosystem and architecture-aware procurement: **10–20 years, possibly longer**.

This is not a delay. It is the nature of the work.

For comparison: the safety architectures that govern aviation, nuclear power, pharmaceuticals, and financial settlement took decades of adversarial pressure, incident investigation, and iteration to reach the conformance maturity that makes them trustworthy today. Governance architectures for artificial intelligence will take the same kind of time, because they are doing the same kind of work.

## Why publish now

Two reasons.

First, the architecture is stable enough to be cited, challenged, extended, and implemented as a reference. The long clock does not start running until the written architecture exists. Publication is how that clock starts.

Second, this is the author's considered view of the architectural pattern AI systems will need in 10–20 years for consequential decision work. The architecture exists because that future is coming whether it is prepared for or not. Publishing the reference now is an act of preparation, not an act of shipping a product.

## What this means for readers

- **If you are looking for production-ready software right now:** this is not that. Implementations exist and are being built openly, but they are prototypes under active development.
- **If you are looking for a reference architecture to assess existing or planned AI systems against:** the 12-level stack is ready now. Use it.
- **If you are building a governed AI system:** the architecture, the messaging protocol, the threshold specification, and the override protocol are all public and free to implement under CC BY 4.0.
- **If you are looking to contribute:** post implementation reports, architecture critique, or scenario stress-tests in [Discussions](https://github.com/bryanunitek/UniCORE-AI/discussions). Translations and adaptations welcome.
- **If you are hoping the author will monetise this:** the architecture is given, not sold. That is not a future plan that might change. It is structural. See [LICENSE.md](LICENSE.md) and [STATEMENT-ON-CLAIMS.md](STATEMENT-ON-CLAIMS.md).
