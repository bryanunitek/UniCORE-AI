# UniCORE-AI Governance: Per-User Data — Regulatory-Purpose-Only and Key Custody

> **Status:** DESIGN COMPLETE — 2026-06-28
> **Layer:** UniCORE-AI (governance / reference architecture) — NOT TrueAI foundation
> **License:** BOTH — public governance doctrine AND binding UniCORE commercial license

## 1. The Rule: AI Regulatory Purposes Only — or a Recognized Court Order

The raw per-user Badge/action data generated by the System (Posting) Badge — the trail that walks the 12-level governed pipeline — is accessible for **exactly two purposes** and no others:

1. **AI regulatory compliance purposes** — the governed AI system consumes it for billing, compliance attestation, and audit.
2. **A Recognized Court Order from the user's jurisdiction** — a court of competent jurisdiction in the country where the user was located when the data was generated.

No tenant opt-in. No internal-management exception. No "cold way." No governed-derived-output side channel that surfaces raw data to a human. The moment the data is used for any other purpose — ranking employees, measuring productivity for dismissal, cost analysis per person — the purpose has changed and the entire legal basis collapses.

### What "Recognized Court Order" Means

A Recognized Court Order is an order from a court of competent jurisdiction in the **user's jurisdiction at the time the data was generated** — not the user's current location, not the requesting party's jurisdiction, not the court's own jurisdiction.

This matters because:
- Users change jurisdictions
- Jurisdictions change over time (new laws, new court structures, Brexit, etc.)
- The governing rule is always: *which jurisdiction applied to this person at the moment this data was created?*

A US court order for an EU citizen's data when that citizen was in the EU requires an EU court order — the US court alone is not a Recognized Court for that data. An order from the UK court for a UK employee's UK-generated data is recognized. An order from a US court for that same employee while they were working in Germany requires a German court order in addition.

### Cross-Jurisdiction Rule

If the data spans multiple jurisdictions — for example, an employee who worked in the UK, US, and Germany — court orders are required from **all** jurisdictions in scope. No single jurisdiction can authorize access to another jurisdiction's people's data. The quorum of court orders multiplies; it does not substitute.

The determination of which jurisdictions are relevant is a question of law for the **court** to decide, not UniCORE. UniCORE's role is to enforce the quorum once the court has made that determination. If courts conflict — one says yes, another says no — that is a dispute for the user and the courts to resolve. UniCORE does not adjudicate between competing court orders. **Data stays sealed until the conflict is resolved and a valid quorum forms.**

## 2. Why the Rule Exists: The Badge Is the Trust

"Powered by UniCORE AI · built on the TrueAI foundation" is not a courtesy mark. It is a **promise to the human users inside the firm** — not to the firm's management — that the system watching their work answers only to AI regulatory compliance and never to a manager judging them.

That promise is the product. If it is broken, the badge is a lie, and it cannot be displayed — not because of a UI toggle, not just because of a license clause, but because **the thing the badge certifies no longer exists.** Trust between the human users and UniCORE AI is gone, and the badge with it.

The badge is therefore a **live, revocable trust certificate.** Present = the governed regime is in force. Absent = the trust is broken. Workers see it; they do not need to read the contract.

## 3. Enforcement: License Breach and the Attestation Chain

Using the data outside the two recognized purposes — for example, surfacing raw per-user data to a human manager for retention, ranking, or cost decisions — is a **breach of the UniCORE commercial license.** It is not a technical violation; it is a contractual violation with real consequences:

1. **License breach** — the tenant has violated the conditions under which they were granted the right to use UniCORE.
2. **Badge revocation** — they forfeit the right to display "Powered by UniCORE AI · built on the TrueAI foundation."
3. **Visible signal** — because the badge is worker-visible, its removal is the visible announcement that the governed regime is no longer in force.
4. **Independent violation** — continuing to display the badge after breach is a second, compounding violation (false attribution / trademark misuse) on top of the license breach.

> **License boundary:** The use-restriction lives in the **UniCORE commercial license** (Solution layer), not in the CC BY 4.0 gift layer. CC BY 4.0 is irrevocable and cannot carry use restrictions. Do not conflate the two.

## 4. How the Rule Is Made Structural: Encryption and Attested Key Release

"AI regulatory purposes only" is a promise unless it is enforced technically. The enforcement is not pseudonymization, tokenization, or scrambling — those are reversible by whoever holds the mapping table. The enforcement is **encryption with hardware-backed key custody.**

### The Hard Constraint

The governed AI process must read the data in plaintext to do its job — to walk the 12 levels, compute compliance, meter billing. But **no human may ever read it** — not tenant administrators, not UniCORE operations staff, not anyone.

This kills the obvious answers:
- Tenant holds the key → tenant admin can decrypt → the "Cold Way" is trivially available. Fails.
- UniCORE holds the key → we can read it → the promise is just our word. Fails.

### The Design: Confidential Computing with Attested Enclaves

The solution is hardware-backed confidential computing:

1. **Envelope encryption at rest** — per-user data keys (DEK) are wrapped by a key-encrypting key (KEK) held in an HSM. Each named human's data is sealed under their own key.
2. **Plaintext only in an attested secure enclave** — the data is decrypted only inside a secure enclave (e.g. Intel TDX/AMD SEV or equivalent) running a **certified UniCORE-AI governance build**. The HSM releases the DEK only when the enclave cryptographically proves it is running the certified code.
3. **No human ever receives plaintext** — the governed AI process inside the enclave produces compliance attestations, billing totals, and other structured outputs. Humans receive only those outputs, never the raw stream.

### The Attestation Roots in the Existing Chain

The HSM's key-release attestation is not a new, separate trust root. It uses the **same chain already used for the "Powered by UniCORE AI" badge** and for the compile-time governance floor:

- **MANIFEST.json SHA-256** — the cryptographic fingerprint of the certified build.
- **`IAiGovernanceCompileTimeFloor.HardOnLevels`** — the binary↔version HardOn pairing that binds a binary to its governance version.

The enclave attests: "I am running the binary whose fingerprint matches MANIFEST.json SHA-256, with the HardOn floor including Level 1 (Truth) and all required governance levels." The HSM releases the key only on successful attestation.

This creates **one root of trust serving three jobs:**
1. Badge validity
2. Compile-time governance floor
3. Key-release gate

The fail-closed property is inherited: uncertified or tampered build → attestation fails → no key released → cannot decrypt on the honest path.

### Per-Person Data Keys

Each named human's data is encrypted under their own key, not a per-tenant master key. This means a lawful decryption event (under a valid Tier 1/2/3 quorum — see below) exposes only the data of the specific person under inspection. The rest of the firm's workforce remains sealed. This is data minimization made structural.

"Trust is the load-bearing wall" — the whole system only works if human users trust the AI system enough to work inside it. Per-person keys are how that trust becomes architectural.

### Key Lifecycle: Retain Then Crypto-Shred

When a person's BO User is deactivated (they leave, change roles, etc.), their data key is:
1. **Retained** — sealed, still decryptable via the proper quorum, for exactly as long as the per-jurisdiction regulatory and legal retention obligation requires.
2. **Crypto-shredded** — when that retention clock expires, the key is destroyed. The data becomes permanently undecryptable by anyone, including lawful courts that arrive later. GDPR storage-limitation and right-to-erasure are achieved structurally: destroy the key, not the data.

The BO User record itself is **never deleted** — the 1U1C accountability spine requires that a Person's identity and their audit trail persist. Only the encrypted activity data under that identity expires on a lawful clock.

## 5. The Three-Tier Quorum: When Raw Data May Be Rendered Readable

The only legitimate times raw per-user data may be decrypted — ever — are:

### Tier 1: Routine Regulatory Inspection

**Who:** L12 Human Governor (tenant) + Regulator/Auditor credential
**UniCORE role:** Absent — normal regulatory access happens without UniCORE operations touching the key.
**Both parties required; neither alone.**

### Tier 2: Break-Glass / Last Resort

**When:** Tier 1 cannot form — the tenant's L12 Governor is unavailable (ill, deceased, firm collapsed, compromised), or a regulator compels access and the tenant will not or cannot cooperate.
**Who:** UniCORE M-of-N key-escrow share (at least M of N custodian shares presented) + Regulator/Auditor credential
**UniCORE role:** Last resort only. UniCORE can never decrypt alone — its escrow contribution is a genuine M-of-N Shamir-style secret split. A role flag is not sufficient; at least the required threshold of distinct, valid shares of the same secret must be presented. No single custodian, and no single share, can reconstruct UniCORE's leg.
**Consequence:** If neither UniCORE operations nor a Regulator can be reached — data is **undecryptable.** This is the cost of the guarantee. It is chosen consciously.
**This is not a flaw. It is the point.**

### Tier 3: Lawful Court Order

**When:** A Recognized Court Order compels access to data for a legal proceeding.
**Who:** Recognized Court Order (from the user's jurisdiction at the time of data generation) + Regulator/Auditor credential.
**Court's role:** Provides the lawful authority and determines which jurisdictions are implicated.
**Regulator's role:** Co-signs as the executing inspectional leg.
**UniCORE role:** Executes the decryption only after the court has determined jurisdiction. UniCORE does not adjudicate which courts have authority.
**Cross-jurisdiction:** If multiple jurisdictions are implicated, court orders from all relevant jurisdictions are required. Courts conflict → data stays sealed until resolved.

### Never Valid

- Any single party alone
- The tenant's management line as a decryption leg
- UniCORE acting alone

All decryption events — across all three tiers — are **append-only logged** under Invariant 8. Tier 2 break-glass events carry a louder audit signal than Tier 1 routine inspections.

## 6. Where the Keys Live: UniCORE-Owned HSM on GVB

The HSM holding the KEKs — and therefore controlling key release — runs on **UniCORE's own infrastructure on the GVB private cloud**, not on a hyperscaler KMS, not on tenant infrastructure.

This is not an accident. The whole design premise of UniCORE is that governance infrastructure belongs to the governed, not to a third-party cloud provider. The keys that make "Powered by UniCORE AI" true run on UniCORE's own metal.

Owning the HSM is about **where the root of trust sits**, not **who can read.** The HSM still releases keys only to the attested enclave; owning it does not create a UniCORE read path. UniCORE operations staff cannot decrypt data any more than a tenant admin can.
